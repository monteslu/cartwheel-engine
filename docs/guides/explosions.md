# Explosions and distortion

{{name}} bends the picture the way a blast does. Heat haze above a fire, a shockwave ring racing across a wall, a glass shard or a water drop that shows the scene through it: all of them draw "move the picture this much here" instead of colour, and one pass after the 3D scene reads the frame through those moves. It looks the same on GL and WebGPU.

Header: [cartwheel/explosions.h](header:explosions.h). Node types: [Shockwave3D](node:Shockwave3D), and [GPUParticles3D](node:GPUParticles3D) with the new `DISTORT` and `REFRACT` blends. Sample: `fx_distort`. Gates: `fx-distort` and `fx-distort-regions`.

## Heat haze

Set a particle system's `blend` to `DISTORT`. Its particles stop drawing colour; each one shifts whatever is behind it by `distort_strength` (in world units, at the particle's distance, so a haze looks the same near and far) times the alpha of its `color_ramp` over life. Without a texture each particle wobbles by its age; a texture's red and green channels (0.5 = no push, like a normal map) give the direction instead, and its alpha the weight.

@include samples/fx_distort/assets/main.cwscene#Haze

A tall column of slow, big, overlapping particles over a fire is heat haze. Keep `distort_strength` small (0.05 to 0.15): the picture should shimmer, not melt.

Two rules keep the picture honest. A haze particle is hidden wherever scene geometry is nearer than it, so a pole standing in front of the haze stays sharp. And the pass never pulls in colour from geometry nearer than the haze, so a foreground object does not smear into it.

## A shockwave

A [Shockwave3D](node:Shockwave3D) is an expanding ring that bends the picture: no texture needed. `max_radius` is where it ends, `duration` how long it takes, `thickness` the width of the bent band, `strength` how far it pushes at the start (the ring slows as it grows and fades out). It fires when it enters the tree (`autostart`); `cw_shockwave_fire` fires it again, so one node can serve every blast: move it to the blast, then fire it. `ground` lays the ring along the node's XZ plane for a blast spreading over the floor; without it the ring faces the camera.

@include samples/fx_distort/assets/main.cwscene#Wave

The ring advances with the fixed step only, so the radius at tick N is the same on every host: `cw_shockwave_radius` reads it.

## Seeing through particles

Set `blend` to `REFRACT` and each particle shows the scene through a lens: the engine copies the scene colour once before the particles draw, and the particle samples that copy bent radially (or by its texture's red and green) by `distort_strength`. Use it for glass shards, water drops, a heat bubble. A sample that would land on geometry nearer than the particle is not taken, so nothing in front smears into the lens. Refracting particles show the scene as it was before any particle drew, so list them first in the tree if other particles should appear on top of them.

@include samples/fx_distort/assets/main.cwscene#Lens

## Cost and presets

The distortion buffer is drawn at a share of the frame that follows the effects preset (half on Low and Medium, full on High and Ultra) and filtered up; colour fringing along bent edges is High and Ultra only. The passes run only while something distorts: a scene without haze pays nothing. Everything here is render only and reads the frame's own depth; nothing reaches the simulation.

## Testing

`tests/distort.mjs` renders `fx_distort` on both backends with the effect on and off and checks that the haze, ring and lens change the picture inside their footprints, that nothing changes outside them, that a pole in front of the haze is identical with and without it (and bends when the pole stands behind it: the control), and that GL and WebGPU agree.

## Flipbooks that look like more frames than they have

A flipbook is a sheet of frames played over a particle's life (`texture`, `hframes`, `vframes`). Sixty-four frames played over a second and a half still step. Give the system a `motion_texture` and the engine blends each frame into the next along baked motion vectors: it draws the frame in between by sliding each frame's picture along its own motion, so 64 frames look like a continuous billow. `motion_strength` scales the vectors (1 = as baked).

`tools/fx/bake-flipbooks.mjs <dir>` (it also writes `scorch.png`, the four scorch shapes) writes the engine's own fire and smoke sheets (8 by 8 frames of 128 pixels, with the motion sheet of each), generated by noise carried along a known flow: no source images, nothing to license. The motion sheet stores, in red and green, how far each pixel moves into the next frame (0.5 = still, scaled by 0.12 of a frame). Bake your own the same way, from any simulation that knows its flow.

## Sub-emitters: smoke from flying debris

Set a system's `emission_shape` to `PARENT` and name another [GPUParticles3D](node:GPUParticles3D) in `parent`: each new particle is born at a living particle of the parent (picked by the same deterministic hash that picks everything else) and can carry a share of its velocity (`inherit_velocity`). Debris that trails smoke, sparks that leave puffs, a rocket's exhaust are all one parent and one child. Put the parent before the child in the tree, and start both in the same tick (an [Explosion3D](node:Explosion3D) does). The parent's particles are read in world space.

@include samples/fx_explosion/assets/main.cwscene#DebrisTrail

Same seed, same tick, same particles on GL and WebGPU: the `fx-sub-emitters` gate checks that every child is born at a parent particle on both.

## One node for a whole explosion

An [Explosion3D](node:Explosion3D) fires everything under it. Its children are the parts, authored like any scene: particle systems with `emitting` off and `one_shot` on, shockwave rings with `autostart` off. Firing it (when it enters the tree, or with `cw_explosion_fire`) restarts them all and adds three things a scene file cannot:

- A flash. A pooled point light flares at the blast and dies away quickly. The pool is small and shared by every explosion of the tree: the effects preset sets its size (2, 4, 8 and 8 lights from Low to Ultra) and a busy scene takes the oldest flash back, so a chain of blasts, or four split-screen views, never grows the light count. After the flash the light is off, and the frame is exactly the frame without it.
- A scorch mark on the ground under the node: a dark layer that fades after `scorch_life` and a glowing layer that cools from orange to nothing over `scorch_time`. Each mark is one of four ragged shapes (`scorch_texture`, a 2 by 2 sheet the baker writes), turned about the vertical and stretched a little at random, so a yard of blasts does not repeat. Twelve marks are pooled; a thirteenth recycles the oldest.
- A shake. `cw_explosion_shake(tree, position)` returns how hard the ground shakes at a place (each explosion adds its `shake_amount` fading with distance and age) and `cw_shake_offset(shake, tick, max_offset)` turns that into a deterministic camera offset. The engine never moves your camera: you decide how much of it a player feels.

@include samples/fx_explosion/assets/main.cwscene#Boom

Everything runs on the fixed step, so the same ticks give the same flash, mark and shake on every host. Stand the node at ground level (that is where the mark lands), and give a hero blast a `duration` that covers its longest part.

## Split screen

Particles simulate once a frame and draw once per view; the flash light is one light all views share; the distortion buffer, the scene copy and the shockwave rings are per view and cost the view's own pixels, so four views cost about what one full-size view does. `tests/boom-perf.mjs` reports the numbers.
